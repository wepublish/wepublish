---
title: Audience Engagement replaces banners
lead: Banners, pop-ups, newsletter and membership prompts and content gates now live together in the new Audience Engagement section, where each rule can grow with how many articles a reader has read.
actionRequired: false
---

**Audience Engagement** in the sidebar replaces **Banners**. A rule decides whom it is for, where it shows and what it asks of readers, in steps such as:

- from the first article: a banner
- after 3 articles: a pop-up asking to sign up for the newsletter
- after 5 articles: the article ends after a few blocks until the reader registers, becomes a member or gives their email address

To start, pick a template (grow the newsletter, promote memberships, show an announcement) and adjust it. Before you save, an overview shows what readers will see and when. If two rules compete for the same spot, the editor tells you which one is shown first and lets you show yours first instead.

**Your existing banners were moved over** as announcement rules, with the same texts, buttons, pages and audiences. Old banner links lead to the new rule.

**Newsletter sign-up without an account:** a step can ask readers only for their email address, right in the prompt. The address is signed up through your Mailchimp integration (Settings → Integrations) and has to be confirmed by email.

**Paywalls** are now found under Audience Engagement too; the Articles menu still links to them. A paywall can now be enforced on the server: readers without access then only receive the first blocks of the article. Update your websites before turning that on.

**Block templates in prompts:** instead of writing a step's text, you can pick one of your block templates (pop-ups, boxes in the article and the cutoff; banners keep their texts). The title and buttons stay.

If a rule asks readers for their email address but sign-ups can't reach Mailchimp, the rule form tells you why and links to the integration.
